## How to use this file — 12 minutes, in pairs

1. One of you reads the **participant** lines out loud. The other runs the interview *from
   `capture.html`*, typing as they go. Do not read the interviewer lines — improvise them.
2. Stop at the end. Compare your block against **"What the block should say"**.
3. Read **"What this scenario is teaching"** last, not first. The point is to be caught out.

Then go send the real messages. **Rehearsal that does not end in a sent message was procrastination.**

## RS1 — The clean one *(H5 → H1, in that order)*

**Setting:** Library ground floor, afternoon. 3rd-year Nursing student. English.

> **Q1 — last file, which class?**
> "A case study report for Adult Nursing. Word doc, but we had to submit PDF."
>
> **Q2 — walk me through what you actually did.**
> "I finished it on my laptop about ten at night. Exported to PDF, went to upload it, and the
> LMS said the file was too big. It's 20 megabytes because I put in photos of the ward
> observation charts. So I googled 'compress PDF', used the first site, it made it 8 megabytes,
> still too big. The limit was 5. Did it again on the same site and it said I'd used my free
> tasks for today and to come back tomorrow or sign up."
>
> **Q6 — did anything go wrong? what did you do?**
> "I made a new account with my other email to get more free tasks. Third time it came out
> 4.6 megabytes and I submitted it at like eleven forty."
>
> *(interviewer follows the door: "How did you know when it was small enough?")*
> "I just kept uploading it to the LMS until it stopped complaining. The site doesn't tell you,
> it just gives you a smaller file and you hope."
>
> **Q3 — devices?** "Laptop the whole time."
> **Q4 — how long?** "Nearly an hour. Most of it was the compressing."
> **Q5 — most annoying part?** "Not knowing how small was small enough."

### What the block should say

```
### RS1                          ← a real one would be S1
- Role: 3rd-year Nursing, MFU
- Channel: in person
- Pains stated (their words, not ours):
  - Not knowing how small was small enough — the tool gives a size, the LMS gives a verdict
  - Free tasks ran out on the second try, at 11pm, with the deadline that night
  - Made a second account with another email to get past the daily cap
- Verbatim quote: “I just kept uploading it to the LMS until it stopped complaining.”
- Hypotheses touched: H5, H1
```

### What this scenario is teaching

**The pain is not the one they named.** Asked directly (Q5), they said *"not knowing how small
was small enough"* — that is **H5**, and it is real. But the second-account workaround in Q6 is
**H1**, it is worth more, and *they never called it a problem.* They described it as a solution.

> **Rule: a workaround reported cheerfully is still a pain.** People do not narrate their
> coping strategies as suffering. Write down what they *did*, not only what they complained about.

Note also what the interviewer did **not** do: no "isn't it annoying when there's a size limit?"
The limit came from the participant, unprompted, which is what lets H5 take a tick.

## RS2 — The one you must abandon *(screener fail)*

**Setting:** Harbor Student Center, lunch. 1st-year, Chinese programme.

> **Screener (2) — in the last month, have you submitted a file for a class?**
> "Mm, not really? Everything this term has been on paper or in class. We had a quiz on the
> LMS but I just clicked the answers."

**Stop here.** Thank them, do not continue, and **write nothing.** Not a block, not a partial
block, not a note "for later".

### What this scenario is teaching

**A non-interview is a correct outcome, not a failed one** — and it is the single most likely
thing to go wrong tonight when you are four short with two hours left.

The temptation at 22:00 is to keep going anyway: *"OK but imagine you had to submit a PDF…"* That
produces a fluent, plausible, completely worthless answer, because the person is now **inventing**
— and an invented answer written into `results.md` is fabricated evidence whether or not you
intended it. **The instrument fails safe only if you let it.**

> **Four real interviews and an honest count of four beats five where one was imagined.** The
> gate criterion is a floor, and a false floor collapses under exactly the question Dr. Prasara
> is most likely to ask: *"tell me about one of them."*

## RS3 — The generaliser *(the pull-back, and what it costs)*

**Setting:** LINE DM to a friend at another university. Thai, translated.

> **Q2 — walk me through what you actually did.**
> "Usually I just use whatever site comes up first. Normally it's fine. Sometimes it's slow.
> Students always have this problem lah."
>
> **Interviewer:** *"Got it — but let's stay with the most recent one. What happened that time?"*
>
> "…Last week. It was a video, a 3-minute presentation for English class. My phone recorded it
> in 4K so it was 900 megabytes and Google Classroom wouldn't take it. I tried two websites,
> both wanted me to make an account before downloading. Then my friend sent me an app, I
> installed it, it put a watermark across the middle. In the end I re-recorded the whole
> presentation at lower quality."
>
> **Q4 — how long?** "The re-recording was 40 minutes because I kept messing up. The rest,
> maybe an hour of trying things."

### What the block should say

```
- Pains stated (their words, not ours):
  - 4K phone video too large for Classroom; no way to make it smaller without an account
  - Two sites required sign-up before download; a third watermarked the output
  - Re-recorded the entire presentation at lower quality — 40 minutes — instead of compressing it
- Verbatim quote: “In the end I just recorded it again, smaller.”  [translated]
- Hypotheses touched: H1, H7
```

### What this scenario is teaching

**The first answer was worth nothing and the second was the best pain in this file.** "Students
always have this problem" is a *belief*; the re-recorded presentation is an *event*, with a
duration, a file size, and a decision.

One sentence — *"let's stay with the most recent one"* — is the difference. It is the highest-value
sentence in the whole instrument, and it is the one people forget under time pressure because it
feels rude. It is not rude. They will answer it.

**H7 also ticks here**, and nobody asked about it: they went looking for a *video* tool and hit
the same wall as the PDF people. That is the cross-media claim, raised unprompted, which is
exactly the evidence standard the promotion rule wants.

## RS4 — Custody, over chat, in Burmese *(H2 — the one the architecture rests on)*

**Setting:** Messenger. Burmese-speaking, 2nd-year IT. Interview run in Burmese; quote translated.

> **Q1 — last file you had to hand in?**
> "Not a class one — for a scholarship application. They wanted my transcript and my passport
> page as one PDF."
>
> **Q2 — walk me through it.**
> "I photographed both with my phone. Then I needed them as one PDF, so I searched and used an
> online JPG-to-PDF site. Uploaded both photos, downloaded the PDF, sent it in."
>
> **Interviewer follows the door:** *"Where do you think that file went after you uploaded it?"*
>
> "…I don't know actually. It's on their server somewhere I suppose. The site said files are
> deleted after two hours. I remember reading that."
>
> **Interviewer:** *"Did you do anything differently because of what was in it?"*
>
> "I waited until I got back to my dorm to do it. I didn't want to do my passport on the campus
> wifi. And I deleted the file from the website after — there was a delete button. But I don't
> know if that does anything really."
>
> **Q5 — most annoying part?** "Honestly the not knowing. I still think about it sometimes."
>
> *(door: scanned handouts)* "Also the scholarship form itself was a scan, in Thai, and I
> couldn't copy the text out to translate it. I typed the whole thing into Google Translate by
> hand."

### What the block should say

```
- Role: 2nd-year IT, MFU · Language: MY
- Pains stated (their words, not ours):
  - Uploaded a passport page and transcript to an unknown site to merge them into one PDF
  - Changed behaviour because of the contents: waited for dorm wifi rather than campus
  - Pressed the site's delete button but does not believe it did anything
  - Thai-language scanned form: retyped the whole thing by hand to translate it
- Verbatim quote: “I pressed delete, but I don’t know if that does anything really.”  [translated]
- Hypotheses touched: H2, H6
```

### What this scenario is teaching

**This is the interview the whole product is waiting for, and it hinges on two probes, not on Q1–Q6.**

The participant volunteered nothing about custody. They described a routine task in neutral terms.
Both custody findings came from the follow-ups in `chat-script.md` §4:

| Probe | What it produced |
|---|---|
| *"Where do you think that file went?"* | They had **read** the deletion promise and could not verify it |
| *"Did you do anything differently because of what was in it?"* | **Behaviour changed** — waited for dorm wifi. That is the pain, in action |

Note what is *not* claimed: they never said "I want a client-side tool", and if they had, it would
be worthless — a hypothetical (`field-kit.md` §4). What they gave instead is a **behaviour**, which
is evidence, and one sentence of genuine unease, which is the quote.

**H6 arrives free**, from the door about the scanned Thai form. This is why the Burmese-language
DMs matter more than the corridor tonight: H6 is the least-evidenced hypothesis in the spec and
this cohort is the only one that raises it without being asked.

## RS5 — The one that contradicts you *(H9 — record it anyway)*

**Setting:** Reply to a 2-star review left on a competitor. 4th-year Business, another university.

> **Q2 / Q5 — what happened, most annoying part?**
> "I use iLovePDF for everything, all the time. The free one was annoying — two files a day and
> then it stops. But they do a student thing, you verify with your university email and you get
> Premium free for a year. I did that in first year. Now it's completely fine, I have never had
> a problem since."
>
> **Interviewer (the H9 door):** *"Do you pay for any file tool now?"*
>
> "No, it's free for me. That's why I use it."
>
> **Interviewer:** *"Earlier you mentioned scanning your ID for the visa office — would you use
> it for that too?"*
>
> "Yeah, I did use it for that. Why not? It's a big company, it's not some random website."

### What the block should say

```
- Pains stated (their words, not ours):
  - Free-tier daily cap was a real problem — until the student plan removed it entirely
- Verbatim quote: “No, it’s free for me. That’s why I use it.”
- Hypotheses touched: H1 (historic — resolved for them by the student plan)
- CONTRADICTED any hypothesis?  H2, H9 — a recognised brand is trusted with an ID scan without
  hesitation, and a free student plan fully removed the cap that drove them. Custody was not a
  consideration; price was, and it is already solved by the competitor.
```

### What this scenario is teaching

**This participant is the most valuable one in the file, and every instinct will tell you to
discard them.** They are "off-message", they like the competitor, and they undercut the thesis.

Both `interview-guide.md` §5 and `results.md` say the same thing, in advance, in writing:

> *H9 is the one that can falsify our value proposition. Record that answer honestly even when
> it hurts.*

That line was written **before** any data existed, and it exists precisely so that this moment is
not a judgement call at 23:00 on gate day. **Write it in the contradictions table, under the
name of the hypothesis it damages.**

And note it does not sink the project — it *sharpens* it. Read carefully, RS5 says brand
recognition substitutes for verification ("it's not some random website"), and that a competitor
has already neutralised price. If that repeats across two participants, the honest response is to
narrow the claim: UniLab's argument is **verifiability**, not free-ness, and the target user is
the one who *cannot* verify and knows it (RS4) — not the one who trusts by brand (RS5).

> **A gate submission that reports one contradiction reads as research. One that reports five
> confirmations and no contradictions reads as marketing** — and it invites the exact question
> you least want: *"did anyone disagree with you?"*

## The five failure modes, in one table

Read this on the way to the interview.

| # | Failure mode | The move |
|---|---|---|
| RS1 | The real pain is described as a solution | Write down what they **did**, not only what they called a problem |
| RS2 | No recent event, and you keep going anyway | **Stop.** Record nothing. Four honest beats five with one invented |
| RS3 | They generalise | *"Let's stay with the most recent one — what happened that time?"* |
| RS4 | Custody never comes up on its own | *"Where do you think that file went?"* → *"Did you do anything differently?"* |
| RS5 | They contradict the thesis | Record it **prominently**. It is the most valuable block you will write |
