# Chat script — running the interview as messages, not as a conversation

**Course:** 1305493 · **Company:** DIGITAL IMPOSTERS PVT. CO., LTD. · **Product:** UniLab
**Written:** 8 Sep 2026 (gate day) · **Serves:** B16 · **Bound by:** LR7

---

## Why this file exists

`interview-guide.md` is written for a person standing in front of you, and `field-kit.md` is the
phone version of the same thing. Both assume a **voice**. On gate day the realistic channel is
**a DM**, and a spoken script pasted into a chat window fails in three specific ways:

1. **Six questions in one message gets one answer.** People reply to the last thing they read.
2. **Silence does the work in speech, and nothing in text.** "Let them reconstruct the sequence"
   has no chat equivalent unless you deliberately send the next question *after* they answer.
3. **The consent line gets skipped** because it reads like a paragraph of terms, so people scroll
   past it. In chat it must be its own message, and it must be answered before anything else.

So: **one question per message. Wait for the answer. Then send the next.** A chat interview takes
about 20 minutes of elapsed time but roughly 4 minutes of your attention, which is why five of
them can run *in parallel* tonight and five corridor interviews cannot.

> **If they answer in chat, that is the interview.** It counts. `field-kit.md` §1b already
> establishes this from the instructor's own `M1-choosing-your-problem` guide: a real user needs
> only to (1) have the problem and (2) be reachable. A DM is reachable.

---

## The order — five messages before you ask anything

| # | Send | Wait for |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The recruiting message (§1) | Any reply at all |
| 2 | The consent line (§2) — **its own message** | An actual "yes" / "ok" / "ได้" / "ရပါတယ်" |
| 3 | Screener (§3) | Two yeses |
| 4 | Q1 (§4) | Their answer |
| 5 | Q2 … Q6, one at a time | Their answer, each time |

**Do not send Q2 until Q1 is answered.** The whole value of the instrument is that Q2 and Q6
reconstruct a real sequence; batching them turns the interview into a survey and the answers into
generalities you cannot cite as a pain.

## 2. Consent — send as its own message, wait for a real yes

### English
> Before I start: I'll note your year and programme, the problem you describe, and maybe one
> sentence you say — no name, no student ID, no contact details. Nothing goes to anyone outside
> my project team. You can stop any time or skip any question, and you can tell me to delete a
> note. Is that OK?

### Thai
> ก่อนเริ่มนะคะ/ครับ ผมจะจดแค่ชั้นปีกับสาขา ปัญหาที่คุณเล่า และอาจจะประโยคเดียวที่คุณพูด —
> ไม่เก็บชื่อ รหัสนักศึกษา หรือช่องทางติดต่อ หยุดตอนไหนก็ได้ ข้ามข้อไหนก็ได้
> และบอกให้ลบได้ตลอด โอเคไหมคะ/ครับ

### Burmese
> မစခင် ပြောပြပါရစေ — သင့်ရဲ့ ခုနှစ်တန်းနဲ့ ဘာသာရပ်၊ သင်ပြောပြတဲ့ ပြဿနာ၊ ပြီးတော့ သင်ပြောတဲ့
> စာကြောင်းတစ်ကြောင်းလောက်ကိုပဲ မှတ်ထားပါမယ်။ နာမည်၊ ကျောင်းသားနံပါတ်၊ ဆက်သွယ်ရန်
> အချက်အလက် ဘာမှ မမှတ်ပါဘူး။ ဘယ်အချိန်မဆို ရပ်လို့ရပါတယ်၊ မဖြေချင်တဲ့ မေးခွန်းကို ကျော်လို့ရပါတယ်။
> ရပါသလားခင်ဗျ?

**A "yes" to the recruiting message is not consent.** They agreed to talk; they have not yet been
told what you write down. Send §2 separately and wait. If they never answer §2, you have no
interview — do not write a block for them.

## 3. Screener

> Two quick ones first — (1) are you studying at MFU right now? (2) In the last month, have you
> had to submit a file for a class — a PDF, a photo, a video, a recording?

Not at MFU is **fine** (see `field-kit.md` §1b) — record where they study instead. **No to (2) is
not fine.** Thank them and stop. Everything below depends on a recent, real event they can
actually reconstruct.

---

## 4. The six questions, as messages

Send **one at a time.** The parenthetical is *your* note, not part of the message.

| # | Send this | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Q1** | "Think of the last file you had to hand in — what was it, and for which class?" | Anchors everything to one real event |
| **Q2** | "Can you walk me through what you actually did? From the file existing to it being submitted." | **The big one.** Most pains live here |
| **Q3** | "What device did you start on, and what did you finish on?" | Phone→laptop switches are a pain nobody reports unprompted |
| **Q4** | "Roughly how long did that take, start to finish?" | Gives you a number to cite |
| **Q5** | "What was the most annoying part?" | Their word for the pain, not yours |
| **Q6** | "Did anything go wrong that time? What did you do about it?" | **The other big one.** The workaround *is* the pain |

If they generalise — "usually I just…" — send: *"Got it — but let's stay with the most recent
one. What happened that time?"*

### Follow the door they open

| They mention | Send |
|---|---|
| a website or app | "How did you pick that one? Has it ever stopped you partway — made you wait, pay, or sign up?" |
| a size limit | "How did you make it smaller? How did you know when it was small enough? How many tries?" |
| a photo being rejected | "What did the form actually say? How did you get around it?" |
| a transcript / ID / certificate | **"Where do you think that file went after you uploaded it?"** then *"Did you do anything differently because of what was in it?"* |
| a scanned handout | "What do you do when you need to quote from one of those? What language was it in?" |
| doing it every week | "Walk me through those steps — same every time?" |
| paying, or a student discount | "Do you pay for any file tool now? Has anyone offered you a free student plan?" |

**The last row is H9, and it can falsify our value proposition.** If they say price is why they'd
switch, custody is not our differentiator. Write that down exactly as they said it.

---

## 5. Close

> Last two — if you could change one thing about handing in files at MFU, what would it be? And
> is there anything I should have asked and didn't?

Optional, and **only** here:
> We're building a free tool for this. Can I send you a link next week and ask what you think?

**Do not send the link now.** A person who has just been shown UniLab will tell you UniLab is
useful, and that answer is worth nothing at the gate — it contaminates every answer above it,
retroactively.

---

## 6. LR7 on a chat channel

A DM is not less regulated than a corridor. Concretely:

- **Never screenshot the chat.** Not for the team group, not for the log, not "just to remember".
- **Never paste the transcript into an AI tool** — including this one. Type the pain in your own
  words into the capture sheet.
- **Never commit** a username, handle, display name, phone number, or profile link.
- Write the block from memory + your own notes, then **close the chat**. The block is the record.
- If they send you the actual file — a transcript, an ID scan — **do not open it and do not save
  it.** Say thanks, and write one line about what it was.

Participants stay `S1`, `S2`, `S3`… and the mapping from code to person is never written down
anywhere, including in a private message to a teammate.

## 7. Writing it up

Use **`capture.html`** — open it on your phone, answer the fields, tap *Copy block*, paste into
`results.md`. It has no name field by construction, so an LR7 violation is not something you can
accidentally type into it.

If you're at a laptop, `/capture-requirement` does the same thing *and* re-points the spec and
backlog citations in the same pass.

**Write the quote within one minute of finishing.** It is the part that decays fastest — leave it
ten minutes and you will paraphrase it into what you were hoping to hear.
